# Journey Map

1. Confirm the ordered steps, actors, short labels, and target slide.
   Use supplied content rather than inventing a six-step process.
2. Measure the canvas and preserve its aspect ratio, title, citations, and
   unrelated content. Create a new slide or replace only an approved region;
   do not duplicate a slide and delete everything except its title.
3. Lay out a dashed horizontal wavy path behind the content, with numbered
   nodes and cards alternating above and below it. Use native editable shapes
   and curves where supported. Keep arrows and numbers clear of text.
4. Follow the deck theme. Optional colors: navy `1E2761` headings,
   blue `2F6FD0` nodes, muted `616A82` body text, pale `EFF3FB` lower cards,
   `D5DEEF` borders, and `9DBCEA` path. Check contrast on the actual background.
5. Size cards and gaps from the number of steps and available width. Six cards
   are an example, not a requirement. Use multiple slides or a simpler flow
   when the content cannot fit with readable labels.
6. Reserve a consistent illustration slot at each card's top if art is requested,
   followed by the heading and short body. Fit images proportionally without
   cropping. A standalone illustration recipe: flat business vector art,
   thin navy/blue lines, white background, no embedded text, consistent style,
   non-sensitive subject, and meaningful alt text.
   The separate `slide-illustrations` skill is optional for more precise fitting;
   this journey layout must remain usable without it or image generation.
7. Aim for at least 14 pt body text. Do not reuse the original 8.5 pt labels.
   Ask before changing meaning to shorten a step.
8. Inspect path continuity, step order, card spacing, image fit, reading order,
   and editability. Report any simplifications or unsupported capabilities.
